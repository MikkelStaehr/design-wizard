UX laws 12

Order follows the moments of building a UI. Dashed cards are the ones I would cut first. SEED marks the four ids the fixtures use.

fittsMUSTSEED

Fitts's law

The time to hit a target depends on its distance and its size.

When it applies
Any screen with controls a user clicks or taps.
Rule, as exported
Make every interactive element at least 44px by 44px.
Check
min-target-size
minPx 44px range 24px to 64px
Tester measures the box of every visible control at each viewport.
hickMUSTSEED

Hick's law

The time it takes to decide grows with the number of choices.

When it applies
Screens where the user picks one action from several.
Rule, as exported
Limit primary actions to 1 per screen.
Check
max-count
max 1 range 1 to 3
Tester counts the visible matches on each screen.

Seed change: range 1 to 3 (was 0 to 5). Zero primary actions is not a useful screen-wide rule.

wcag-contrastMUSTSEED

Contrast minimum (WCAG 2.2, 1.4.3)

Text needs enough contrast against its background to be read with low vision.

When it applies
Every screen with text.
Rule, as exported
Give all body text a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against its background.
Check
contrast
minRatio 4.5:1 range 4.5:1 to 7:1
Tester computes each text colour against its effective background.

Seed change: minimum 4.5 (was 3). Body text below 4.5:1 fails WCAG AA.

wcag-focus-visibleMUST

Focus visible (WCAG 2.2, 2.4.7)

Keyboard users must be able to see which element has focus at every moment.

When it applies
Every screen with links, buttons or form fields.
Rule, as exported
Show a focus outline at least 2px thick on every interactive element reached by keyboard.
Check
focus-visible
minOutlinePx 2px range 1px to 4px
Tester tabs to each match and measures its outline or ring.
response-limitsMUST

Response-time limits

A response within 0.1 s feels instant; after about 1 s users notice they are waiting.

When it applies
Every control that changes something on screen when it is activated.
Rule, as exported
Show a visible response within 100 ms of every button press.
Check
response-time
maxMs 100 ms range 50 ms to 1000 ms
Tester clicks each match and times the first visual change.
recognition-recallSHOULD

Recognition over recall

Recognising an option on screen is easier than remembering it from an earlier step.

When it applies
Multi-step forms, filters and any screen that depends on an earlier choice.
Rule, as exported
Keep earlier choices visible on every step that depends on them.
Check
manual
Reviewer answers yes or no: On each later step of a multi-step form, are the choices made in earlier steps visible without going back?
consistencySHOULDCUT FIRST 3

Consistency and standards (Jakob's law)

Users expect the same thing to look and work the same way everywhere, as it does in products they already know.

When it applies
Products with more than one main screen.
Rule, as exported
Keep the main navigation in the same place and the same order on every screen.
Check
manual
Reviewer answers yes or no: Moving between the main screens, does the main navigation stay in the same place in the same order?

Why cut first: A shared layout component gives this almost for free; it rarely fails in practice.

proximitySHOULD

Law of proximity (Gestalt)

Things placed close together are read as one group.

When it applies
Forms, settings and any screen with groups of related fields.
Rule, as exported
Group related fields by spacing, with more space between groups than inside them.
Check
manual
Reviewer answers yes or no: On each form, is the gap between groups of fields larger than the gap between fields inside a group?
von-restorffSHOULDCUT FIRST 2

Von Restorff effect

Among similar items, the one that differs is the one people notice and remember.

When it applies
Lists and tables where one item needs attention, such as an error or an overdue date.
Rule, as exported
Make the item that needs attention differ by more than colour: add a word, an icon or weight.
Check
manual
Reviewer answers yes or no: In a list with a flagged item, such as an overdue row, can you still find that item with the screen in greyscale?

Why cut first: Largely covered by wcag-contrast plus the Part A guardrail that colour never carries meaning alone.

error-recoveryMUST

Help users recover from errors

An error message helps only if it says what went wrong and how to put it right.

When it applies
Every form and every action that can fail.
Rule, as exported
Show each error next to its field, saying what went wrong and what to do next.
Check
manual
Reviewer answers yes or no: When a required field is left empty on submit, does the message next to that field say what to enter?
goal-gradientSHOULDCUT FIRST 1

Goal-gradient effect

People speed up and drop out less when they can see how close they are to the goal.

When it applies
Flows with three or more steps, such as checkout, booking or onboarding.
Rule, as exported
Show the current step and the total number of steps in every multi-step flow.
Check
manual
Reviewer answers yes or no: On each step of a multi-step flow, does the screen show its position, such as Step 2 of 4?

Why cut first: Narrow: only multi-step flows, and peak-end already covers how flows end.

peak-endSHOULDSEED

Peak-end rule

People judge an experience mostly by its most intense moment and by its end.

When it applies
Flows with a clear end, such as booking or sign-up.
Rule, as exported
End every main flow on a screen that confirms the result and offers one next step.
Check
manual
Reviewer answers yes or no: At the end of the main flow, does the last screen say in plain words what happened and offer one next step?
